excerpt: |
  World order is changing significantly, yet the International Monetary System remains with the US dollar as irreplaceable world reserve currency.  But dollar dominance could well be a &quot;red herring&quot;. The world should be largely indifferent to which currency—or hegemon—is dominant, since any core power could use the system to weaponise economic interdependence in similar ways. What matters more are the distributional consequences of the system. Any IMS delivers core benefits—efficiency, liquidity, and support for global growth—but also entails persistent costs at the periphery, including inequality, structural imbalances, and exposure to coercive economic measures. In this view, what has changed is not the existence of such trade-offs, but how they are distributed and experienced under evolving geopolitical conditions.
